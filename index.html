ProPose: Topology-Unified 2D Pose Estimation across Intact, Residual and Prosthetic Limbs

Abstract

Driven by the availability of large-scale datasets, Human Pose Estimation (HPE) plays a critical role in numerous downstream tasks. However, mainstream benchmarks exhibit severe representation bias, predominantly featuring able-bodied individuals. While a few pioneering datasets have attempted to address limb differences, their annotation protocols fail to generalize, struggling to represent specialized mechanical structures like running blades or unprosthetized residual limbs. To bridge this gap, we introduce ProPose, a large-scale benchmark featuring a novel annotation protocol that unifies the topological representation of biological limbs, diverse prostheses, and physical absences within a single framework. Because real-world prosthetic images are inherently scarce and exhibit extreme long-tail distributions, we design a Real-to-Synthetic data expansion pipeline to explicitly synthesize and expand the underrepresented cases.

However, simply training existing models on this enriched dataset often leads to suboptimal solutions, as they estimate each keypoint independently and might hallucinate non-existent joints on mechanical structures. To resolve this, we propose ProLoss, a structure-aware objective that enforces keypoint dependencies within a single limb to prevent unrealistic limb predictions. Extensive experiments demonstrate that our approach improves the classification accuracy of long-tail prosthetic joints by 2% to 6% without compromising spatial coordinate localization performance. This work sets a foundation for inclusive pose estimation, unlocking new possibilities for understanding the interactions between human bodies and assistive devices.

Omni-Pose Representation Protocol

One topology for intact, residual, prosthetic and absent limbs — achieved by adding distal extremities and a semantic type to every joint.

Why a new protocol?

Standard protocols such as COCO-17 are designed for able-bodied individuals. LDPose appends 8 residual keypoints to localise the stump, but leaves prosthetic devices unannotated. ProGait labels prostheses by mapping mechanical parts onto biological joints, which works for anthropomorphic devices but breaks down for non-biological geometries: a carbon-fibre running blade simply has no anatomical ankle. None of them can express the difference between a joint that exists in a device, a joint that exists in flesh, and a joint that does not exist at all.

31-keypoint topology

  • 17 standard body keypoints — the COCO topology (head, torso, limb joints).
  • 8 residual keypoints — the physical endpoint of the stump at each amputation level, inherited from LDPose.
  • 6 distal extremities (new) — fingertip, heel and toe, left and right. They anchor the end of the kinematic chain and the effective contact points, so a prosthesis is described consistently no matter what its mechanical design looks like.

Semantic type: the second annotation axis

Every keypoint additionally carries a semantic type \(c \in \{\texttt{Biological}, \texttt{Prosthetic}, \texttt{Absent}\}\), which is what makes a single topology able to describe every limb configuration:

Intact

A joint realised by the body itself, on an anatomically complete limb. Labelled Biological.

Residual

The physical endpoint of a stump. Also Biological — flesh, not a device — and told apart from an intact joint by keypoint ordering.

Prosthetic

A joint realised by a mechanical device — a prosthetic elbow, a blade, a myoelectric hand. It has a coordinate, but it is not anatomy.

Absent

A joint that physically does not exist, and therefore carries no coordinate at all. The kinematic chain simply ends where the limb ends.

Limb joints (including the new distal points) admit all three types; residual keypoints admit only Biological or Absent; core joints such as head and torso are universally Biological. The attribute is assigned per joint, not per limb: a biological wrist can be Absent while the fingertips of the prosthetic hand attached below it are Prosthetic.

Visibility protocol

Conventional visibility flags describe whether a coordinate can be observed, not whether a joint exists. When a residual limb is clearly visible and no prosthesis is worn, the absence of the subsequent joints is itself an observable anatomical fact. We therefore give such keypoints a visible flag (\(v=2\)) together with the Absent type: the absence is visible and deterministic, even though no coordinate is annotated.

Mitigating the long tail

Prosthetic joints are not merely rare, they are rare in a structured way: the further up the limb the amputation, the fewer the examples. Relative to the LDPose image subset annotated under the same protocol, the prosthetic share of elbow keypoints rises from 0.19% to 5.81% (a 31× increase) and that of wrists from 1.00% to 8.63% (8.6×), while the already better-represented lower-limb joints change by at most 1.5×. The expansion is concentrated exactly where the tail was thinnest. The test set is expanded as well: prosthetic elbow and wrist keypoints grow from 38 to 129 and from 135 to 457. Across amputation levels, ProPose adds at least 1,400 annotated residual keypoints, and 6,770 at the above-elbow level.

Annotation quality and ethics

  • Two independent expert annotations. Every image is annotated by two biomechanics experts; a third expert resolves disagreements. 94.0% of samples reach direct agreement, 5.8% are resolved after third-expert review, and 0.2% are discarded as unresolvably ambiguous.
  • Cascade de-duplication. MD5 hashing removes exact duplicates, perceptual hashing (Hamming distance ≤ 5) clusters near-duplicates, and the clusters are reviewed by hand.
  • De-identification. Where copyright or licensing status is unclear, faces are replaced with a synthetic, non-existent identity, removing biometric features while preserving pose.

Real-to-Synthetic Data Expansion

Web crawling alone cannot fix a 0.2% class. The pipeline generates the missing configurations — and throws most of them away.

  1. Phase 1 — Data collection. After de-duplication and manual screening, 1,807 high-quality real-world images are obtained. 1,370 are used as seeds for generation; the remaining 437 (covering 499 unseen subjects) go into the test set.
  2. Phase 2 — Instruction-driven image-to-image synthesis. Contextual outpainting turns close-up crops with truncated limbs into half- or full-body images; pose editing and balancing alters the subject's posture from text prompts that explicitly declare which joints may bend and which prosthetic components are rigid, with targeted oversampling of rare categories such as prosthetic elbows and knees.
  3. Phase 3 — Quality and privacy assurance. Three annotators inspect each generated image and discard visual artifacts and kinematic implausibilities. Roughly 62% of generated images are discarded; 9,558 are retained for training. No generated image enters the test split, so evaluation is performed entirely on real photographs.

Which editor? A quality-control study

Before fixing the editor used in the pipeline, we generated 100 candidate images with each candidate model and manually screened them against three task-specific criteria: whether the pose is clearly edited, whether the original prosthetic or residual-limb condition is preserved, and whether severe visual artifacts are present. A candidate is accepted only if all three hold.

ProLoss: a Structure-Aware Objective

Keypoints on one limb are not independent. A residual elbow forbids a biological wrist below it — and the loss should say so.

Problem formulation

We formulate Omni-Human Pose Estimation as joint coordinate regression and semantic attribution. For an input image \(I \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W \times 3}\) the model predicts \(K\) keypoints

$$\mathbf{p}_i = (\mathbf{x}_i,\, s_i,\, c_i), \qquad \mathbf{x}_i \in \mathbb{R}^2,\; s_i \in [0,1],\; c_i \in \mathcal{C} = \{\texttt{Bio}, \texttt{Pros}, \texttt{Abs}\}.$$

For keypoints labelled Abs the coordinate has no physical referent, so coordinate supervision is masked out while the type label is kept for classification.

Distribution-aligned supervision

The imbalance between biological and prosthetic/absent joints is severe enough that a plain cross-entropy is dominated by the majority class. We apply a decoupled inverse-square-root weight to the classification branch only:

$$\omega_{i,c}^{cls} = \frac{1/\sqrt{N_{i,c}}} {\frac{1}{|\mathcal{C}_{valid}|}\sum_{j \in \mathcal{C}_{valid}} 1/\sqrt{N_{i,j}}},$$
$$\mathcal{L}_{reg} = \frac{1}{K}\sum_{i=1}^{K}\mathbb{1}(v_i>0 \ \&\ c_i^* \neq \text{absent})\, \ell(\mathbf{p}_i,\hat{\mathbf{p}}_i), \qquad \mathcal{L}_{cls} = -\frac{1}{K}\sum_{i=1}^{K}\mathbb{1}(v_i>0)\,\omega_{i,c_i^*}^{cls} \sum_{c \in \mathcal{C}} y^*_{i,c}\log \hat{y}_{i,c}.$$

Spatial regression is unaffected by the semantic long tail, and our ablation confirms that re-weighting it actively hurts — so the weighting stays on the classification branch alone.

Bio-Contrastive Boundary Loss

Each limb branch admits exactly one anatomically valid terminal state. For an annotated residual keypoint \(r\) we define its mutually exclusive set \(\Omega_r\) of keypoints on the same limb branch that cannot coexist with it, and contrast the biological confidence of \(r\) against them:

$$\mathcal{P}_r = \frac{\exp(p_r^{\text{bio}}/\tau)} {\exp(p_r^{\text{bio}}/\tau) + \sum_{j \in \Omega_r}\mathbb{1}(v_j>0)\exp(p_j^{\text{bio}}/\tau)}, \qquad \mathcal{L}_{bio} = -\frac{1}{|\mathcal{R}|}\sum_{r \in \mathcal{R}} \mathbb{1}(v_r>0\ \&\ c_r^*=\textit{Biological}) \log \mathcal{P}_r .$$

Minimising \(\mathcal{L}_{bio}\) raises the probability of the true residual endpoint while suppressing biological predictions for its descendant joints — a kinematic filter against hallucinated limbs. The total objective is

$$\mathcal{L}_{total} = \lambda_{reg}\mathcal{L}_{reg} + \lambda_{cls}\mathcal{L}_{cls} + \lambda_{bio}\mathcal{L}_{bio},$$

with \(\lambda_{reg}=1\), \(\lambda_{cls}=0.05\), \(\lambda_{bio}=0.01\) and \(\tau=0.2\) in all experiments. AP and Type-Acc remain stable under moderate variation of these values, so ProLoss needs no task-specific tuning.
